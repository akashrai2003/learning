# Strategy: improve your offer prospects before becoming a different engineer

## The near-term opportunity

You have stronger evidence than a generic 'I built a chatbot' profile: production ingestion, retrieval, model serving, Docker-based deployment, GPU-memory constraints, asynchronous workflows, OCR failure analysis and client delivery. The problem is making the scope legible and independently assessable while passing ordinary software-engineering interviews.

Your pitch should connect a constraint to an engineering action and an observed result. For example: limited accelerator memory required changes to cache configuration and request batching; an offline deployment required reproducible artifacts and recovery workflows. Include exact numbers only where you can defend their measurement and are permitted to disclose them.

The new phone and C++ curriculum add a distinctive direction. They should not erase the production experience that can earn the next offer. In the next four months, an employer is more likely to trust a well-tested Python platform contribution plus a detailed incident analysis than a claim of kernel expertise after a few tutorials.

## Role map

| Role family | Fit now | Evidence to emphasize | Main gap to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applied AI / AI Engineer | Strongest initial lane | Retrieval, extraction, evaluation, backend integration and delivery | Python coding, evaluation rigor, system design |
| Backend Engineer — AI platform | Strong adjacent lane | APIs, queues, storage, retries, model integration and operations | SQL, concurrency, testing and service reliability |
| ML Platform / Inference Platform Engineer | Selective but credible | vLLM operations, benchmarking, containers, GPU debugging and residency | Source-level changes, observability and larger-scale architecture |
| AI deployment / forward-deployed engineer | Useful alternate lane | Client delivery, on-prem systems, requirement discovery and integration | Travel expectations, communication and commercial scope |
| Inference performance engineer | Stretch where platform-focused | Measured latency/memory work, profiling and a runtime contribution | Defensible methodology and source-level depth |
| CUDA kernel / compiler specialist | Longer-term selective lane | Future C++/SIMD/kernel artifacts | Direct systems programming and kernel optimization evidence |
| AI developer tools / technical solutions engineer | Optional lane | Debugging, examples, developer communication and OSS | Verify coding depth, compensation and actual role mix |

Do not reject a role solely because it says 'Senior' if the described scope and experience fit. Conversely, do not pursue a 5–8 year distributed-kernel role simply because you know its model names. Read must-have requirements and distinguish preferred qualifications. Use approximately 1–3 years as primary and flexible 2–4 year roles as adjacent; treat much higher requirements as explicit exceptions.

## Two parallel markets

Use a starting application allocation of roughly **50% global remote, 40% India/Bengaluru, 10% exceptional stretch**. These percentages are a planning choice, not an evidence-based conversion guarantee. Review after four weeks. If global geography exclusions dominate, shift more time to employers already able to hire in India. Keep some international exploration even while progressing domestic interviews.

For international roles separate: a company headquartered in the US/EU; a team based there; a role requiring residency there; and a company hiring in India. Only the last determines whether your current location is eligible. 'Remote Europe' or 'EMEA' is not an India-eligible label. A global EOR provider's presence does not prove a particular employer will use it.

## The positioning sentence

Draft: 'I build production AI systems under deployment and resource constraints: document intelligence, Python services, local LLM serving and evaluation. I am deepening inference-runtime and edge deployment skills through reproducible benchmarks and open-source work.'

Use this as an editable description of direction. Do not describe yourself as a kernel engineer, distributed-serving expert or open-source maintainer without corresponding work. Be precise about personal ownership versus team output.

## Your evidence inventory

For each major work item write: problem, constraints, your responsibility, decisions, alternatives, implementation, validation, failures and outcome. Suggested stories: model residency and switching; throughput/context tradeoffs; OCR disagreement handling; ingestion and retry design; air-gapped upgrade process; evaluation or grounded-answer failures.

For the reported aggregate throughput improvements, recover prompt lengths, concurrency, model/quant, runtime version, generation counts and measurement boundaries. Distinguish aggregate output tokens/s from a single user's speed. If those records cannot be shared, reproduce the concept publicly with a new benchmark rather than turning uncertain private numbers into public claims.

## Success measures you control

You control screened opportunities, evidence-backed applications, coding practice, completed experiments, thoughtful contributions and interview reviews. You do not control reach, maintainer response time or offer timing. Track both effort and conversion so you can change strategy instead of merely increasing volume.

By week 4 aim to have coherent public evidence and active applications. By week 8 aim to have diagnosed interview weaknesses and a reviewable contribution or equivalent technical artifact. By week 12 aim to be progressing serious processes. By week 16 aim to evaluate offers or make a clearly informed pipeline adjustment. These are operating milestones, not promises that external events happen on schedule.
