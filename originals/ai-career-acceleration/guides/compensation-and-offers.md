# Compensation targets, qualification and offers

## Define the target precisely

You currently describe approximately 20 LPA. Moving to 35–40 LPA would be a 75–100% increase in headline annual compensation, but the useful comparison depends on fixed cash, variable pay, equity and benefits. Your international target is USD 80–100K annual gross. Neither target is a prediction of what a particular employer will pay.

For India decide whether 35–40 LPA means guaranteed fixed cash or total CTC. If you prefer fixed cash, say so explicitly in your private criteria and recruiter conversations. A package including a large one-time bonus or speculative equity may have a much lower recurring amount.

## Employee versus contractor

Compare annual guaranteed cash, expected variable cash, paid time off, insurance, retirement contributions where applicable, equipment, on-call/overlap requirements, contract duration, termination terms and payment reliability. A USD contractor invoice amount is revenue before your own costs and obligations; do not compare it directly to employee take-home.

For arithmetic only: USD 80K–100K spread over 2,000 billable hours is USD 40–50/hour; over 1,000 billable hours it is USD 80–100/hour. These are not market rate recommendations or guarantees of available work. Unpaid gaps and nonbillable time can materially change annual revenue. Check details with qualified advisers before signing a cross-border arrangement; this guide does not calculate taxes or provide legal advice.

## Qualify early

Ask for salary range, currency, base/variable/equity breakdown, employment route, India eligibility, required overlap and assessment process. A US salary band in a US-only posting is not an India offer. Published ranges may cover several levels or geographies. If pay is unknown, leave it unknown in the tracker.

## Expectation wording

India draft: 'For this move I am targeting approximately 35–40 LPA, with a strong preference for recurring fixed compensation. I would like to understand the role's level and the fixed, variable and equity breakdown.'

International draft: 'For an India-eligible remote engineering role with this scope, I am targeting approximately USD 80–100K in annual guaranteed gross compensation, or a contract structure evaluated separately for hours and benefits. What range is budgeted for this location and level?'

Adapt to the role and your genuine boundaries. You can ask the employer's range before naming a number. If the application requires current compensation, provide accurate information; do not fabricate offers or salary history.

## Negotiate from role value

Use the team's scope, evidence of fit and real alternatives. Explain why you can own the expected work. Do not rely solely on a percentage increase over current CTC, and do not claim a competing offer that does not exist. Ask whether there is flexibility in recurring base, joining support, review timing, work arrangement or level if the total is constrained.

## Offer comparison

Build a side-by-side record: guaranteed annual cash; variable target and conditions; one-time payments; equity type/vesting/liquidity; benefits; hours/overlap; manager/team; engineering scope; on-call; notice/start date; stability and learning. Keep one-time cash separate from recurring compensation. Treat private startup equity as uncertain rather than spendable annual salary.

For foreign currency, use a dated verified exchange rate only when you need an INR comparison. This pack intentionally avoids a live conversion so it does not turn a stale rate into a compensation claim. Evaluate sensitivity to currency movement if a real offer arises.

## Decision process

Write your minimum acceptable conditions before final rounds, then revisit with actual offer details. A lower headline domestic offer can be attractive if fixed cash and role quality are strong, but you should make that tradeoff deliberately. An international contract can be attractive without being equivalent to a stable full-time role. No universal winner can be selected without the actual terms.

Do not resign based on a verbal range, an unfinished contract or an unverified recruiter claim. Confirm the real offer, start conditions and your obligations before making irreversible decisions. This is practical job-transition planning, not a promise of legal protection.
