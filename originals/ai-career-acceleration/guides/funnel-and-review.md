# Funnel review and adjustment rules

## Track states consistently

An opportunity is not an application. Use discovery, qualified, applied, screen, technical, final, offer and closed. Record geography rejection, seniority mismatch, pay mismatch, no reply, technical rejection, withdrawal and accepted alternative separately. An application and a conversation with the same company belong to one coherent record.

For weekly conversion, use comparable cohorts and allow response lag. Ten applications from yesterday should not be compared with ten from a month ago. A small sample can suggest a problem but rarely proves a precise conversion rate.

## Useful metrics

Count qualified applications, screens, technical rounds, final rounds and offers. Track source and role family so you can tell whether referrals, direct applications, India roles or global remote roles produce progress. For content, track relevant conversations and evidence visits when observable. For OSS, track reproductions, tests, reviewable patches, reviewer feedback and merged work, without treating merge count as the only measure.

## Diagnostic table

| Observation | Investigate first | Next two-week adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Many geography exclusions | India eligibility and arrangement | Narrow to explicit India/worldwide roles; qualify ambiguity early |
| Few responses from good-fit roles | Resume clarity, level, evidence and application age | Review two resume variants and lead evidence; keep a controlled application cohort |
| Screens but no technical progression | Pitch, scope, compensation and role fit | Rehearse concise ownership stories and qualify expectations |
| Coding failures | Pattern, implementation speed, tests and explanation | Add focused independent practice on the failed category |
| Design failures | Requirements, estimates, tradeoffs and failure reasoning | Record two structured design mocks and repair weak sections |
| OSS stalls before a patch | Scope, hardware and setup friction | Choose a smaller reproducible surface or backup repo |
| PR waits for review | Maintainer capacity and submission quality | Respond to existing feedback; continue owned project; avoid repeated bumps |
| Posts get views but no relevant signal | Audience, specificity and evidence | Narrow topics and improve repository/report links |
| Final rounds but weak offers | Level, team budget, negotiation and alternatives | Broaden qualified pipeline and compare recurring compensation |

## Week 4 review

Are materials accurate? Is the flagship scope still small? Have you applied before finishing every skill? Is the primary contribution feasible on your hardware? If not, narrow immediately. Do not spend a second month building a broad tool that no role needs.

## Week 8 review

Identify actual interview bottlenecks and double down on those. Decide whether global eligibility or skill fit is the international constraint. Shift application allocation accordingly. Review content effort: if it is consuming hours without evidence, reduce frequency and improve the underlying work.

## Week 12 review

Prioritize active high-fit processes. Keep a maintenance level of discovery so one rejection does not empty the pipeline. Reduce new project scope and preserve mock/review time. Prepare compensation questions using real role ranges and constraints.

## Week 16 review

If there is an offer, compare terms and role quality. If not, diagnose stage-by-stage: missing eligible opportunities, poor response, technical conversion or final-stage issues. Extend the appropriate part of the plan for four weeks rather than restarting every activity. A difficult market outcome is information, not proof that the salary ambition or your ability is invalid.

## Sustainable execution

Keep a small weekly scorecard and one adjustment. Do not chase every new model or rewrite your profile after every rejection. Protect current job performance, sleep and enough recovery to interview well. This is an intensive transition plan, not a requirement to work indefinitely at maximum capacity.
