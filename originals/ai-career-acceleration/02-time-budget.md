# Time budget: one working plan alongside a full-time job

## Do not add two complete curricula together

The earlier 24-week study pack assumes roughly 17 hours a week. This career plan uses approximately **17.5 total hours a week**, including selected inference/C++ study. For the next 16 weeks, extend the previous learning calendar rather than doing both plans in full. Day numbers do not need to advance together.

Suggested weekly allocation: coding/interview foundations 4.5 hours; public project or OSS 4.5 hours; applications and relationship-building 3 hours; content/profile 1.5 hours; targeted C++/inference study 2.5 hours; review 1 hour. Total 17.5 hours. Technical project work also develops inference skills, so the separation is a planning aid rather than a perfect accounting taxonomy.

## A sustainable week

| Day | 150-minute session |
|---|---|
| Monday | 60 minutes coding, 60 minutes project/OSS, 30 minutes search |
| Tuesday | 60 minutes targeted C++/inference, 60 minutes interview/design, 30 minutes applications |
| Wednesday | 90 minutes project/OSS, 30 minutes preparing a technical post, 30 minutes relevant conversation |
| Thursday | 60 minutes coding, 60 minutes project/OSS, 30 minutes applications |
| Friday | 60 minutes targeted learning, 45 minutes mock/incident review, 45 minutes search and follow-up |
| Saturday | 60 minutes project/OSS, 45 minutes design/coding review, 45 minutes publishing and genuine replies |
| Sunday | 60 minutes funnel review, 30 minutes technical recap, 45 minutes next-week preparation, 15 minutes backlog cleanup |

This is a default pattern; the weekly files override it when there is a deliverable or interview. Optional 30-minute extensions must stay within your three-hour ceiling. If you have only two hours, remove optional work and reduce post frequency before cutting active interview preparation.

## During interview-heavy weeks

Treat a 60-minute interview as part of the day's learning time, and reserve another 20–30 minutes for preparation/debrief. Reduce content and new OSS scope first. Keep existing reviewer responses timely within your availability. Do not begin a large new contribution while handling final rounds.

## What to keep from the original curriculum

Keep compilation/types/ownership fundamentals; one stable model; the benchmark runner; Python reference operations; cache reasoning; numerical tests; and small native deployment exercises. These support interviews and public proof. Slow down the full educational engine and advanced Android integration if the offer pipeline needs attention.

Do not claim completion of the 24-week inference curriculum in 16 weeks through this combined plan. You are selecting high-value exercises and extending the rest. Resume the deeper schedule after the transition or when your weekly capacity allows it.

## Minimum viable week when work is busy

Complete one timed coding exercise with review; progress one existing application; qualify three roles; make one concrete project/OSS improvement; and write one short experiment note. Publishing can pause. A credible repository and a prepared interview outperform an exhausted content schedule.

## Weekly targets and limits

After the setup fortnight, start with 5–8 carefully screened applications, 3–5 contextual professional conversations, two posts based on real work, one mock segment, and one OSS work item in progress. These are suggested inputs, not required output quotas or expected replies. If researching eligibility consumes the time, prioritize fewer high-fit applications.

Maintain **one primary repo, one public flagship project, and at most one upstream PR in flight initially**. A second repo is a backup for blocked work, not another simultaneous commitment. Do not pay for GPU time before a contribution or experiment requires it and you have set a small explicit budget.
